Executive Overview

A commercial website and a private content management portal, built specifically for Leikah Plant Hire — not adapted from a template.

Leikah Plant Hire now has a complete digital headquarters: twenty-one distinct page types covering every service, machine class, industry and project, all of it presented in a visual identity built from scratch for this business — and all of it editable by your own team without a developer.

The brief was not to produce a brochure. Plant hire and heavy mechanical work are bought on evidence: can this contractor move the volume, hold the haul road, and get the machine running again at two in the morning. So the site was built to answer those questions in the language your customers actually use — survey volumes, rolling resistance, torque and clearance, availability — and to make the next step, a quotation or a breakdown call, never more than one click away from wherever the visitor happens to be standing.

Everything a client could reasonably want to change is content, not code. Service descriptions, machine specifications, project records, photographs, FAQs, contact details, opening hours, the emergency notice — all of it lives in a database and is edited through a private portal built for this business. Nothing meaningful is hardcoded. When the fleet grows or a rate changes, you update it yourself, and it appears on the site immediately.

What makes this different from a template

Most small-business websites are a purchased theme with the colours changed. This one has a designed identity: a chamfered steel plate mark with a terraced cut through it — the profile of an open-cast bench, which is literally what your earthmoving division builds. That single silhouette then repeats through the whole interface: the corner treatment on buttons and panels, the shape of the section dividers, the favicon, the loading screen.

The letterforms in LEIKAH are drawn as vector paths rather than set in a font, so the wordmark is pixel-identical everywhere it appears — browser tab, printed quotation, vehicle vinyl, site board. You own a real mark, not a font choice.

Two things worth knowing up front

The site is currently deployed as a public preview so you can review every page on any device. In that preview, enquiry forms display and validate but do not send — sending switches on with the full hosting deployment. Separately, a handful of content areas were deliberately left empty rather than filled with invented material: client testimonials, client logos, safety certificates and job vacancies. Those are claims only you can make, and every part of the site that uses them handles the empty state gracefully. Sheet 11 lists what is still needed before launch.

The Homepage Experience

Nine sequences that take a visitor from "who is this" to "request a quotation" without them noticing they were being led.

The homepage is composed as a single cinematic scroll. Each section hands off to the next, the brand is present throughout without ever shouting, and the two commercial actions — get a quote, call the breakdown line — are reachable from every screen height.

No headline, no paragraph, no button — the name is the message. Underneath, a single line of positioning and four trust indicators.
SEQUENCE 02The partner markThe ISM mark sits low in the hero, small, quiet and clearly labelled as an association rather than a call to action. It signals depth of backing without competing with the Leikah identity.
SEQUENCE 03The propositionThe descriptive copy that used to crowd the hero now sits here, in a calm reading measure: who Leikah is, where it operates, and what it does. This is where a visitor confirms they are in the right place.

SEQUENCE 05The fleet showcaseThe signature section. A vertical rail of six machine classes; select one and a full-bleed plate of that machine slides in, stamped like a technical drawing (PLATE 03 / 06) with a four-column specification strip beneath it. It is the closest thing on the site to walking the yard.
SEQUENCE 06The proof bandOperating figures counting up as they enter view — years in operation, machine classes on hire, response commitment. Numbers set in the technical typeface, aligned on their digits.
SEQUENCE 07How we workThe five-stage engagement walkthrough: site walk and scope, method and safety file, mobilisation, production and maintenance, survey and handover. Each stage carries its real timing label — "Day one", "Week one", "Close-out". This is the section that removes purchasing risk.

What the visitor feels

Motion is used to direct attention, never to entertain. Sections rise gently into place as they are reached; background imagery drifts a fraction slower than the page scrolls, which reads as depth; fine dust particles move across the hero at very low opacity; a surveyor's grid sits faintly behind the darker sections. Every one of these effects is disabled automatically for visitors who have asked their device to reduce motion — a real accessibility requirement, not a checkbox.

The Services Showcase

Thirteen capabilities, three divisions, twenty-four hand-drawn icons — and a structure that grows without a developer.

The service system is the commercial engine of the site. It is built as a library rather than a list, so a visitor can enter at any level — a division, a single service, a machine class, an industry — and always find their way to a quotation.

Three divisionsEarthmoving & Plant Hire, Heavy Mechanical, and Supply. Every service belongs to one, which is what allows filtering, cross-linking and sector matching to work automatically rather than being maintained by hand.
A custom icon familyTwenty-four icons drawn specifically for this business on a shared grid, with a common ground line and a single stroke weight, so an excavator, a grader, a transmission and a hydraulic hose all look like members of one family. No stock icon set contains a motor grader or a final drive that reads correctly at small size — so they were drawn.
Proof points, not adjectivesEach service carries four short claims written the way a site manager would say them. "Volumes reconciled to survey." "Hoses made on site." "Equivalents disclosed, never substituted quietly." Specific claims are credible; generic ones are noise.
A visible working methodEvery service sets out its own numbered sequence — the engine overhaul runs receive and strip, clean and crack-test, strip report and quotation, machining and parts, assembly to build sheet, run and release. Showing the method is how a contractor demonstrates competence before winning the work.
Technical illustrationLine drawings of an excavator, a dozer, a hauler and an engine, drawn onto the page stroke by stroke as they enter view, with heavier weights for primary outlines and finer ones for construction lines — the visual convention of a workshop drawing.
Everything cross-linksA service knows its division, its related machine classes, its relevant FAQs and its associated projects. Adding a project in the portal makes it appear on the right service page automatically, with no second step.
Every route ends in a quoteEach service page closes with a quotation route tagged to that service, so enquiries arrive pre-classified and can be routed to the right desk on arrival.
Growing the library

Adding a fourteenth service is a content task, not a development task. Enter it in the portal with its title, division, summary, proof points, method steps, icon and photography, and the site generates its card, its detail page, its cross-links and its search entry. The same is true of machine classes, industries, projects and articles.

Gallery & Media

Your own photography, processed properly, presented as evidence rather than decoration.

Every photograph on the site is Leikah's own. Twenty-six source images were processed into forty-seven optimised renditions so that each one is delivered at the right size for the device requesting it — a phone never downloads a desktop-sized image.

Each image also carries a tiny embedded preview that appears instantly and blurs up into the full photograph as it loads. The result is that the page never jumps or reflows while images arrive, which is both a better experience and a direct search ranking factor.

The galleryTwenty-six images in a responsive layout that reflows cleanly from three columns to one. Category filtering narrows to fleet, site work, workshop or facility, applied instantly without reloading the page.
Full-screen lightboxSelecting an image opens it full-screen with its caption. Arrow keys move between images, Escape closes, and on a phone the visitor swipes. Keyboard focus is held inside the lightbox while it is open and returned to the originating image when it closes — the correct behaviour for anyone navigating without a mouse.
Before & after comparisonA draggable slider that wipes between two states of the same view, used on project records. For terracing, rehabilitation and haul road work this is the most persuasive single element available — it shows the change rather than describing it.
Automatic optimisationImages are converted to modern compressed formats, letterboxing is trimmed, and multiple widths are generated automatically. When you upload a new photograph through the portal it goes through the same pipeline — you never have to think about file sizes.
Captions and alternative textEvery image carries a caption and a description, both editable. The descriptions serve visitors using screen readers and are read by search engines, which is how photography contributes to being found.
Video, ready when you areThe content system already carries video fields for the hero and for project records. No footage has been supplied yet, so none is shown — the moment you have drone or site video, it can be placed without rebuilding anything.
Worth commissioning

The single highest-return addition to this site would be a half-day photography session covering the workshop bays mid-rebuild, the fleet lined up, and the team on site in full PPE. The layouts are already built to hold that material, and people photography in particular is the one thing the current image set does not include.

Departmental routingFive contactable desks are listed by name and function — Breakdown Dispatch, the Plant Hire Desk, Workshop & Component Rebuild, Supply & Procurement, and Accounts & Vendor Onboarding. Each carries its own direct line and address, so a visitor reaches the right person first time.
Live opening hoursThe page reads the current time and states whether the business is open right now, when it next opens, and — separately — that breakdown dispatch is answered around the clock, every day including public holidays. The visitor never has to work out the schedule themselves.

User Experience & Design

A visual language built from the work itself — and engineered to behave on a five-year-old phone at the edge of coverage.

The design direction is called Cut & Section. Its central idea is that everything in the interface takes its shape from the same chamfered plate as the mark, so that the identity is not a logo placed on a layout — it is the layout.

The test applied throughout was simple: if the logo were removed from the corner, would you still know whose site this is? Answering yes required a real system — a palette, a typographic hierarchy, an icon family, background treatments and motion behaviour that only belong to this business.

The paletteDeep ink blacks for weight, a graded steel range for structure and hairlines, and a single machined gold used sparingly for emphasis. Gold is the only accent on the site, which is what makes it mean something when it appears.
Three typefaces, three jobsA wide industrial grotesque for headlines, a highly legible face for reading, and a technical monospaced face for specifications, coordinates, figures and labels. The monospaced face is doing real work — it is what makes a specification strip read as a specification rather than as marketing copy.
Backgrounds that are not flatSurveyor's grids, terrain contour lines, drifting dust, brushed-metal sheen and oversized brand watermarks sit behind the darker sections at very low opacity. They give depth without competing with content, and they are drawn rather than photographed, so they cost almost nothing to load.
Motion with a purposeContent rises into place as it is reached; imagery drifts a fraction slower than the page for depth; figures count up; blueprints draw themselves stroke by stroke; the brand letters assemble on arrival. Every effect directs attention to something. Nothing loops for decoration.
Reduced motion, fully honouredA visitor whose device requests reduced motion gets the entire site with the animation removed and nothing else lost. This is a genuine accessibility need, and the site treats it as a first-class mode rather than an afterthought.
Designed for a phone firstEvery layout was composed at phone width before desktop. Tap targets are sized for gloved hands, text remains legible in sunlight, and no page ever scrolls sideways. Most of your visitors are standing on a site, not sitting at a desk.
Usable without a mouseThe whole site can be operated by keyboard, with a visible focus indicator at every step and a skip link to jump straight to content. Colour contrast was tuned so that even the smallest technical labels clear the accessibility standard against every background they appear on.
Considered transitionsMoving between pages fades rather than flashes. A first visit opens with a short brand sequence. Buttons, cards and tiles respond to touch and hover with restraint — enough to confirm the interaction, never enough to distract.
